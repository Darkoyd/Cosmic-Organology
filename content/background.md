    text: |
      **Sculpture, art direction, curatorship.**
      Facultad de Arquitectura y Diseño, Universidad de los Andes.
      Bogotá, Colombia, 1986.
      [santiago-reyes.com](https://www.santiago-reyes.com/)

      Santiago's work examines knowledge systems used to control and colonize
      nature. His work consists of drawings, installations (large-scale and
      site-specific), moving images, and sculptures. Santiago understands
      these formats as techniques to engage with systems of power and their
      contemporary materiality. His most recent projects take interest in
      today's colonization of space, reflecting on the limits of extractivism
      and the survival of life-forms, within and beyond our planet.

        title: "Regimes of Knowledge Production: a Conversation on the Predicaments of Artistic Research"
        url: https://www.santiago-reyes.com/project/approximating-borders-artistic-research-in-practice/
        by: Ilona Jurkonytė, Manuel Ángel Macía & Santiago Reyes Villaveces. In *Approximating Borders*. Academic paper.
        text: |
          A conversation on the relationship between artistic research,
          scientific knowledge, geopolitics, and the historical legacies of
          the Cold War, questioning how knowledge is produced, validated, and
          circulated, particularly when art and science intersect.

          Its central argument is that research should not be understood as a
          neutral or universal process of knowledge production. Instead, it is
          shaped by historical, political, institutional, and technological
          structures that determine what counts as knowledge, who is
          authorized to produce it, and which histories become visible or
          remain marginalized. The authors use the Cold War not simply as a
          historical period or military confrontation, but as a continuing
          *regime of knowledge production* whose structures still influence
          contemporary institutions, technologies, and ways of understanding
          the world.

          *For Cosmic Organology:* the paper offers a theoretical framework for
          understanding how art and science can come together to produce new
          ways of knowing and experiencing the universe, and explains why
          artistic practice can be an active form of research rather than
          simply a means of communicating scientific discoveries.

      - year: "2022"
        title: Cosmic Sympathy
        url: https://www.santiago-reyes.com/project/cosmic-simpathy/
        by: Solo exhibition. Casanova, São Paulo, 18 June – 23 July 2022.
        text: |
          A body of installations and drawings developed around Stoic
          cosmology and the concept of cosmic sympathy, the idea that all
          bodies and entities in the universe are interconnected through
          forces of attraction, even when they are separated by space or time.
          Gravitational force becomes the central thread connecting scientific
          discoveries, political histories, human perception and artistic
          experience.

          **"I See the Earth":** a red neon sign reproduces Yuri Gagarin's words
          upon seeing Earth from space in 1961, evoking the planet as a shared
          and interconnected place of existence for human and non-human life.

          **Lunar eclipse drawings:** positive and negative drawings based on
          photographs of the 1919 eclipse in Sobral, Brazil, recall the
          empirical confirmation of Einstein's theory of relativity.

          **Goodwill Moon Stone:** a graphite drawing of the lunar fragment
          gifted by Richard Nixon to Brazil's former president, Emílio Médici,
          in 1971, connecting gravity and space exploration with international
          diplomacy, political interests and colonial legacies.

          **The vestibular organ:** an installation reproducing the inner ear's
          vestibular system, the human sensory organ of gravity, introducing
          the body as a site through which gravitational forces are perceived
          and experienced.

          Together, these works construct a non-linear genealogy of
          gravitational attraction. Rather than treating gravity exclusively as
          a physical phenomenon, the exhibition proposes it as a condition of
          interdependence between bodies, objects, environments and the
          universe. The visitor is not simply invited to observe gravity, but
          to enter its field and experience the relationships it establishes.

          *For Cosmic Organology:* this perspective expands the understanding
          of cosmic tensions beyond astronomical phenomena, approaching the
          universe as a network of relationships and interdependencies in which
          seemingly distant elements remain connected through forces that
          exceed human perception.

      - year: "2026"
        title: Gravitational Organology
        by: Santiago Reyes Villaveces. MIT Kavli Institute Artist Residency, 2026.
        text: |
          An artistic research project exploring how we can experience cosmic
          phenomena that exceed the limits of human perception, particularly
          gravitational waves and spacetime disturbances. Drawing on the
          physics of wave propagation, it investigates how seemingly distant
          elements are connected through continuous interactions.

          The proposal consists of a hybrid sculptural instrument that combines
          anatomical and astronomical references, translating scientific data
          and gravitational-wave models into vibrations, sound, light and
          material resonance. Rather than representing scientific phenomena,
          the installation seeks to make them physically perceptible.
          Collaboration with MIT scientists is central: through the exchange of
          research data, simulations and technical knowledge, artistic and
          scientific practices would shape the sculpture's design and
          interactive behaviour. *Gravitational Organology* invites audiences
          to experience the universe not only as something to observe or
          understand, but also as something to feel.
      - year: 2022–2023
        title: Acoustic Fossil, Cartagena
        url: https://www.santiago-reyes.com/project/fosil-acustico/
        by: Santiago Reyes Villaveces & Daniel Villegas Vélez. Baluarte de Santa Catalina, Cartagena de Indias, 24 November 2022 – 23 February 2023. Winner, Túnel de Escape 2022, Ministerio de Cultura.
        text: |
          *Fósil acústico* transforms a space within Cartagena's historic walls
          into an immersive resonance chamber, inspired by the structure of the
          human inner ear. Through a sculptural representation of this organ,
          the installation explores listening, balance and spatial perception
          as ways of understanding our relationship with the environment.

          The work connects Cartagena's colonial history to the contemporary
          climate crisis, highlighting how colonial exploitation and extractive
          capitalism have contributed to environmental transformations that now
          threaten the Caribbean, particularly its coastal communities and
          mangrove ecosystems. Synthesized sounds, recordings of local species
          and field recordings from vulnerable territories, preserving acoustic
          traces of ecosystems that may eventually disappear, are activated
          through an interactive sculpture that lets visitors modify the sonic
          environment, generating unpredictable combinations of resonances,
          noises and silences.

          The installation invites visitors to reflect on climate change,
          environmental loss and collective responsibility through immersive
          and meditative listening, proposing sound as a way of sensing
          ecological transformations.

      - year: "2024"
        title: "Acoustic Fossil: Listening (with) the River"
        url: https://www.santiago-reyes.com/project/fosil-acustico-escuchar-con-el-rio/
        by: Santiago Reyes Villaveces & Daniel Villegas Vélez. Museum of Modern Art of Medellín, 26 April – 1 September 2024.
        text: |
          The project transforms Lab3 into a resonance chamber through a
          tactile sculpture shaped like the inner ear, which allows visitors to
          manipulate the installation's sound environment. Field recordings and
          a continuous synthesized *drone* respond in real time both to the
          audience's interaction with the sculpture and to the activity of the
          waters of the Aburrá River: a monitoring station near the river's
          source, in San Miguel, measures currents and turbulence with
          hydrophones and sensors, and transmits data to the Museum to modify
          the timbre of the drone.

          The recordings include soundscapes from the river basin collected
          through community workshops, and sounds of native species recorded
          over the past twenty years and preserved in the Humboldt Institute's
          Environmental Sound Collection. Each recording becomes an "acoustic
          fossil": a trace left by the interaction between human and natural
          forces, projected into the future as a voice that acknowledges its
          own ephemeral existence.

          As ecosystem disruption and climate change reach irreversible levels,
          every sound also speaks of its imminent disappearance. The work is an
          invitation to feel and think about listening through the body, a
          situated body, and to let sound touch us while offering us the
          opportunity to touch listening itself.
